# Proposed concise abstract — editorial alternative only

Opportunistic occurrence records rarely translate directly into field-search units. We developed an occurrence-conditioned method that reconstructs environmental support across leave-one-prototype-out perturbations, retains a frozen 2.5% tier, and aggregates supported cells into non-ranked, bounded patches using a 1-km same-area complete-link rule. We froze the candidate-generation method before evaluating 96 taxon-region pairs across 12 predefined Japanese regions and 480 spatial folds, retaining failed and empty folds as zero. At a 10-km regional screening scale, mean held-out recovery exceeded same-size random patch sets by 0.08559 (pair-bootstrap 95% CI 0.05119–0.12165; one-sided sign-flip p = 3.33 × 10⁻⁵), with positive mean lifts for plants (0.05702) and animals (0.11415). Geographic transfer did not meet all preregistered gates: a reserved 24-taxon country-framed replication had a confidence interval crossing zero; a fresh 48-taxon confirmation had positive conditional lift but only 34/48 taxa were temporally evaluable, below the required 75%. A subsequent observability test stopped before held-out evaluation following 29 HTTP 429 responses among 3,161 historical provider queries. These findings support retrospective regional screening within the tested Japanese frames, but not calibrated occupancy, exact-site precision, field efficiency, general global validity, or superiority over other survey-design tools. Geographic framing and observation supply require separate evidence and explicit abstention.
