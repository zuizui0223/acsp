# From occurrence records to finite field-survey decisions: cross-taxon validation of occurrence-conditioned regional prioritization

## Status

Working manuscript draft centered on the pre-*Campanula microdonta* cross-taxon validation program. The primary confirmatory evidence comprises independently sampled taxon–region cohorts evaluated using training-only candidate reconstruction, fixed Top-5 decisions, and random Top-5 counterfactuals from identical candidate pools. A later predeclared standard-baseline reconstruction is reported separately as secondary component-attribution evidence. A second untouched 24-pair benchmark directly contrasts the frozen ACSP decision with a production-aligned fitted-SDM Top-5 rule on matched training folds, candidate pools, and budgets. That comparison is used primarily to characterize whether the two methods make the same field decision, not to establish universal superiority of either method. A later one-shot global observability attempt is included only as an auditable provider-supply/evaluability boundary because it aborted before heldout years were opened. Production-only evidence integration, access, routing, field feedback, and later island-method development remain outside this paper.

## Abstract

Biodiversity surveys often begin with incomplete and spatially biased occurrence records, but field teams must ultimately choose a small number of places to visit under a fixed budget. Species distribution models (SDMs) generally return a location-indexed fitted quantity such as relative suitability, intensity, or occurrence probability depending on model design, whereas survey planning requires a finite-set decision. We developed Adaptive Complementarity-based Survey Prioritization (ACSP), an occurrence-to-decision framework that reconstructs regional candidates from training occurrences and selects a fixed-size survey set. We evaluated the frozen ACSP core using repeated spatial-block hold-out across independently sampled taxon–region pairs in Japan. Complete 0.1-degree spatial blocks were withheld, candidate pools were rebuilt from training records only, and candidates or score components directly associated with known occurrence locations were excluded before selection. For every fold, the ACSP Top-5 set was compared with random Top-5 sets drawn from the identical candidate pool. Failed folds and pairs remained in intention-to-evaluate estimates, and inference was conducted at the taxon–region-pair level. Within 10 km, ACSP recovered a mean proportion of 0.0981 of withheld animal observations compared with 0.0572 under same-pool random selection across 12 independently sampled animal pairs, a lift of 0.0408 (95% confidence interval 0.0031–0.0847). Across 36 independent plant pairs, recall was 0.1098 for ACSP and 0.0912 for random selection, a lift of 0.0186 (95% confidence interval 0.0035–0.0374; pair-level sign-flip p = 0.0233). Plant recovery at 5 km was unstable and was not retained as an exact-location claim. In a separately predeclared reconstruction benchmark, local focal-taxon evidence emerged as the principal observed selection signal, whereas a general added benefit of geographic complementarity was not demonstrated. Finally, in an untouched 24-pair matched-pool comparison, ACSP and production-aligned fitted-SDM Top-5 ranking had essentially the same all-declared 10-km recovery (ACSP minus SDM = −0.0003, 95% bootstrap interval −0.0385–0.0367). Despite this similar performance, their decisions were usually different: among 101 SDM-evaluable folds, the two Top-5 sets shared a mean of only 1.93 candidates, mean Jaccard overlap was 0.264, and the complete Top-5 set matched in one fold. A separately preregistered global automatic-framing attempt aborted during historical provider eligibility after 29 of 3,161 queries returned HTTP 429; 2021–2025 heldout data remained unopened, so the hypothesis was unavailable rather than negative. These results support ACSP as an auditable occurrence-to-finite-decision layer whose output is operationally distinct from a fitted SDM surface, while limiting validation to the declared Japanese frame and allowing SDM output itself to serve as an optional evidence source.

**Keywords:** biodiversity survey design; environmental analogue; occurrence data; spatial block cross-validation; survey prioritization; decision support; finite-set selection; species distribution model

## 1. Introduction

Biodiversity surveys are frequently planned from opportunistic occurrence records collected for purposes other than standardized sampling. These records can reveal broad environmental associations and geographic patterns, but they do not directly specify which small set of places should be visited next. Species distribution models address the important task of relating occurrences to environmental predictors and projecting a location-indexed fitted quantity such as relative suitability, occurrence intensity, or probability, depending on model design. A field team faces a downstream decision: given a fixed number of visits, which finite set of regional search locations should be selected?

The distinction is one of estimand and output, not a claim that SDMs are necessarily simple or spatially independent. Modern SDMs can include nonlinear responses, interactions, spatial structure, random effects, and complex ensembles. Their primary fitted object nevertheless remains a location-indexed model quantity. Selecting the five highest-scoring candidate cells is a downstream decision rule applied to that model. ACSP instead declares the finite survey set itself as the decision object: occurrence information, a generated candidate pool, available evidence, and a fixed budget are mapped to a subset of candidates to consider for field survey.

A simple ranking of the highest-scoring cells does not necessarily solve every survey-design objective. Nearby or environmentally redundant cells can consume the budget without broadening coverage. Conversely, purely spatially balanced or environmentally balanced designs can spread effort efficiently but ignore focal-taxon evidence. Complementarity, environmental coverage, spatial balance, active survey design, and SDM-guided survey planning are established ideas; ACSP does not claim to originate them. The methodological contribution evaluated here is their placement in an auditable occurrence-to-decision workflow that reconstructs candidates using training data, selects a fixed-size focal-taxon set, and evaluates the final set against feasible decisions drawn from the identical generated pool.

This distinction matters for validation. Conventional prediction metrics assess a model surface or candidate-level scores. They do not directly test whether a Top-k survey decision recovers more withheld observations than another feasible Top-k decision. Comparing selected sites with random points from an unrestricted landscape also conflates candidate generation with selection: a method may appear successful simply because its candidate envelope contains favorable regions. ACSP instead uses a same-pool counterfactual. Both ACSP and its comparator receive the same candidate locations and budget, so their difference estimates the added recovery value of the declared selection policy within that pool.

Our primary confirmatory question was whether frozen ACSP Top-5 regional decisions recovered more held-out occurrences within 10 km than random Top-5 decisions from the same candidate pools. Candidate surfaces were rebuilt independently in each spatial hold-out fold using training occurrences only. Direct occurrence-location and occurrence-distance evidence were excluded before ranking. Development taxa were separated from independent confirmation taxa, failed cases were not replaced, and repeated folds were summarized within taxon–region pairs. We also assessed whether the result was robust to taxon composition and inference seed, while retaining unsuccessful 5-km analyses as evidence against a general exact-location claim.

Random selection alone cannot identify which component of a survey policy matters. We therefore added a separately predeclared secondary reconstruction benchmark comparing the unchanged ACSP policies with local-evidence-only ranking, geographic maximin selection, environmental maximin selection, and a combined geographic–environmental maximin baseline. Because the original candidate-level fold pools had not been permanently frozen in the repository, this benchmark regenerated occurrences and candidates for the same frozen taxon–region cohorts using a new protocol seed. We consequently treat it as secondary component-attribution evidence rather than as a reanalysis of the exact confirmatory folds.

A further question is whether ACSP is merely a relabeled fitted-SDM ranking rule. To address that question without turning the study into a universal competition between modeling traditions, we predeclared an untouched matched-pool benchmark. A production-aligned fitted SDM and ACSP received the same training fold, survey bounds, candidate pool, Top-5 budget, and 10-km endpoint. We evaluated held-out recovery as a secondary performance contrast, but separately quantified outcome-free decision disagreement using Top-5 overlap, rank association, selected score profiles, and geographic dispersion. This design permits two methods to have similar recovery while still testing whether they make the same operational field decision.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 ACSP decision object and frozen policies

The validated ACSP core takes a focal taxon, occurrence records, a bounded survey region, and a fixed candidate budget. Training occurrences characterize locally relevant environmental or terrain states and generate candidate locations. Let `O` denote occurrence information, `C` the generated candidate pool, `E` the declared candidate evidence, and `B` the candidate budget. ACSP can be represented as a policy

\[
\pi(O,C,E,B) \rightarrow S, \qquad S \subseteq C, \ |S| \le B,
\]

where the output `S` is a finite set of regional search anchors. It is not an estimated occurrence probability and does not guarantee that each representative coordinate is occupied or accessible.

The confirmatory policies were frozen before the independent cohorts were evaluated. Plant candidates were selected by local-habitat evidence alone. Animal candidates combined local-habitat evidence with geographic complementarity, with weights 0.75 and 0.25, respectively. The budget was five candidates. These narrow policies differ from the current production application, which can incorporate additional observed, macro-model, survey-gap, access, logistics, and field-feedback evidence. Those production extensions are not treated as independently validated in this paper.

### 2.2 Taxon–region cohorts

Taxon–region pairs were sampled using predeclared strata for broad taxonomic group, Japanese geographic region, and regional occurrence-record count. Scientific names used during development were excluded from later confirmation draws. Taxa used in an earlier confirmation cohort were excluded from subsequent extensions. Failed taxa were retained in audit tables and were not replaced with taxa that produced more convenient results.

The supported primary confirmatory evidence comprises 12 animal taxon–region pairs from an independent mixed cohort and 36 non-overlapping plant pairs: 12 plant pairs from the mixed cohort and 24 additional independently drawn plant pairs. The development cohort was used to establish and freeze the policies and is not the basis of the primary independent effect estimates.

### 2.3 Spatially honest candidate reconstruction

Occurrences were assigned to 0.1-degree spatial blocks. In each of five repeated folds, complete blocks were withheld. The candidate builder received only the remaining training occurrences and reconstructed the candidate pool and local-habitat evidence without access to held-out coordinates.

Known-location and occurrence-supported candidate types were removed before selection. Direct occurrence support, occurrence density, survey-gap distance, environmental novelty derived from known locations, and distance-to-known evidence were excluded from the confirmatory score. This prevented trivial recovery through candidates placed at or immediately around observations that remained in training. Candidate generation and selection were completed before held-out recovery was calculated.

Missing or failed folds contributed zero to the intention-to-evaluate pair mean. This rule prevented technical or ecological failures from disappearing through complete-case analysis.

### 2.4 Fixed-budget recovery and same-pool counterfactual

For each fold, ACSP selected five candidates. A held-out occurrence was counted as recovered when at least one selected candidate lay within the declared recovery radius. The primary frozen endpoint was recall within 10 km. Results at 2 and 5 km were retained as sensitivity analyses, but a 5-km plant effect that failed to replicate was excluded from the supported product claim.

The central primary counterfactual consisted of 200 random Top-5 sets sampled without replacement from the identical candidate pool. The same-pool design held constant the candidate-generation process, geographic envelope, candidate availability, and survey budget. A greedy oracle using held-out outcomes was computed only to describe theoretical headroom within the candidate pool; because it used outcomes unavailable at decision time, it was not treated as an operational comparator.

### 2.5 Pair-level inference and stability

Repeated folds from the same taxon–region pair were not treated as independent biological replicates. Fold outcomes were averaged within pairs using the fixed five-repeat intention-to-evaluate denominator. Confidence intervals resampled pair means. Pair-level sign flips supplied a randomization test of the mean lift over random selection.

A separate stability audit operated on frozen independent pair-level results without rebuilding candidates or changing the algorithm. Across five random seeds it performed 10,000 pair-level bootstrap draws, 10,000 random half-cohort draws, 10,000 sign-flip draws, and leave-one-pair-out analyses. These procedures evaluated sensitivity to finite cohort composition and inference randomness; they were not substitutes for independent taxon sampling.

### 2.6 Secondary standard-baseline reconstruction benchmark

After the primary confirmation results and claim boundary had been frozen, we predeclared a second benchmark to identify whether the observed selection value was attributable to focal-taxon local evidence, geographic spread, environmental spread, or their combination. The taxon–region cohort membership and ACSP plant and animal policies were unchanged. The comparator protocol fixed Top-5 selection, a 10-km endpoint, 0.1-degree spatial blocks, five repeats, 200 random sets per fold, and a geographic–environmental mixture weight of 0.5 before outcomes were inspected.

The operational comparators were local-evidence Top-5, geographic maximin, environmental maximin after robust scaling, and an equally weighted geographic–environmental maximin. Environmental comparisons used elevation, slope, aspect, roughness, and topographic position index, with aspect represented by sine and cosine. Environmental and dual-space comparisons were restricted by a predeclared input-eligibility rule to taxon–region pairs for which all expected folds contained finite values for all five raw terrain variables. The greedy held-out oracle was retained as non-operational headroom.

The original confirmatory repository snapshot preserved pair- and fold-level recovery summaries but not the full candidate rows required to run new selectors. Therefore, on 24 July 2026 the benchmark re-fetched GBIF occurrences and regenerated candidate surfaces and spatial-block assignments for the same frozen taxon–region cohorts under the new predeclared protocol. Candidate builders again received training occurrences only, and held-out coordinates and recovery labels were attached only after candidate construction. All fold exports were checksum-audited. Because occurrence snapshots and block assignments changed, these results are secondary reconstruction evidence and must not be substituted for the original confirmatory estimates in Sections 3.1–3.3.

### 2.7 Untouched matched-pool fitted-SDM decision contrast

Before the direct fitted-SDM outcomes were generated, we sampled and froze 24 new taxon–region pairs, comprising 12 plants and 12 animals distributed across four geographic strata. Taxa used in earlier development, confirmation, and SDM-related work were excluded, yielding no taxon overlap with the prior 131-name exclusion set and no duplicate taxon within the new cohort. Candidate generation, SDM fitting, and held-out recovery were not performed until after this cohort and the execution contract had been frozen.

The SDM comparator reused ACSP's production automatic-SDM implementation rather than introducing a convenient post-hoc model. Training occurrences were spatially balanced with a maximum of 300 records and subjected to remote-outlier quality control. Five macroclimatic variables were used with 500 generated background points over the training bounding box plus a 20-km margin. Logistic regression, random forest, ExtraTrees, and gradient boosting predictions were combined by equal-weight averaging. The resulting value was interpreted as a relative suitability score for candidate ranking, not a calibrated occupancy probability.

For every spatial hold-out fold, the fitted SDM and ACSP saw the same training information and selected from the same distance-free candidate pool with the same Top-5 budget. Held-out coordinates were attached only after the decision sets had been frozen. The primary administrative estimand retained every declared fold under the existing failure rule; an additional SDM-evaluable analysis restricted both ACSP and SDM to folds in which the fitted SDM produced a valid Top-5 set.

The initial benchmark exposed two implementation-only defects. First, the aggregate verifier matched a top-level `fold_results.csv` file as if it were a fold directory. Second, the candidate table contained an all-missing production placeholder named `sdm_suitability`; merging predicted suitability generated suffixed columns and prevented the intended score column from being read. Both invalid outputs were rejected. Recovery reused the exact original training, held-out, and candidate artifacts from the predeclared run, removed only the empty placeholder before the unchanged production SDM calculation, and preserved genuine shared-candidate or SDM fitting/scoring failures. No GBIF refetch, split regeneration, candidate regeneration, predictor change, threshold change, or non-SDM result change was permitted. All recovered pair artifacts were checksum verified.

The direct performance contrast was treated as secondary. The principal differentiation diagnostics were deliberately outcome-free: the script did not read held-out recovery values and quantified only the decisions and candidate evidence. For SDM-evaluable folds we calculated the number of shared Top-5 candidates, Jaccard overlap, complete-set agreement, Spearman rank association between local evidence and fitted-SDM suitability across candidates, the local-evidence and fitted-SDM-suitability profiles of each selected set, and descriptive mean pairwise geographic distance within each selected set.

### 2.8 Reproducibility and interpretation controls

The publication builder regenerates the original retrospective validation table, seed-sensitivity table, machine-readable claim matrix, the reviewed standard-baseline comparison table, and frozen plant and animal policy manifests. It additionally validates the exact protocol and execution-contract fingerprints and reviewed artifact SHA-256 digests for the fitted-SDM decision contrast, then exports the held-out performance contrast separately from the outcome-free decision-difference table. It validates the terminal record for the later global observability attempt and exports its supply and hypothesis statuses as a separate supplementary table. It does not read field GPS data, post-baseline island algorithms, or heldout global observability outcomes.

### 2.9 Prospective global observability/evaluability gate

To test whether an ordinary taxon-name input could be framed beyond the fixed Japanese regions without hand-drawn bounds, we preregistered a one-shot provider-eligible observability sequence. The sequence first froze an exclusion-filtered historical candidate snapshot across 12 regions, two taxonomic groups and four record-count strata. It then required complete historical provider eligibility for every candidate before selecting 96 frames offline by a response-independent identity hash. Any provider error triggered `abort_not_evaluable`; no substitution, second activation, new seed or heldout access was permitted. The supply gate and the observability hypothesis were declared as separate axes so that failure to construct the frozen denominator could not be reported as a negative scientific result.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Independent animal confirmation

Across 12 independently sampled animal taxon–region pairs, ACSP recovered a mean proportion of 0.0981 of held-out observations within 10 km. Random Top-5 sets from the same candidate pools recovered 0.0572. The mean lift was 0.0408, with a pair-clustered 95% confidence interval of 0.0031–0.0847.

Every leave-one-pair-out mean lift remained positive; the minimum was 0.0258. Across five inference seeds, the minimum bootstrap probability that mean lift was positive was 0.9858, and the minimum corresponding probability among random half-cohorts was 0.9772. The largest pair-level sign-flip p-value across seeds was 0.0853, reflecting the limited power of a 12-pair cohort despite a stable positive direction.

### 3.2 Independent plant confirmation

Across 36 independent plant taxon–region pairs, ACSP recall within 10 km was 0.1098, compared with 0.0912 under same-pool random selection. The mean lift was 0.0186, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.0035–0.0374 and pair-level sign-flip p = 0.0233.

All leave-one-pair-out lifts were positive, with a minimum of 0.0120. Across five seeds, the minimum bootstrap probability of positive lift was 0.9942, the minimum random-half-cohort probability was 0.9913, and the maximum sign-flip p-value was 0.0271. The positive plant direction was therefore not attributable to one pair or one inference seed.

### 3.3 Boundary of spatial precision

The attempted general plant claim at 5 km did not pass independent confirmation. In the additional plant cohort, Top-5 recall at 5 km was 0.0493 compared with 0.0379 for same-pool random selection, but the pair-clustered lift of 0.0114 had a 95% confidence interval of −0.0067–0.0298. The effective candidate-cell width was also too coarse for a general exact-location interpretation. ACSP therefore retains 10 km as its supported regional scale and treats 5-km results as sensitivity evidence only.

### 3.4 Secondary comparison with local, geographic, and environmental selection baselines

The secondary reconstruction benchmark completed successfully for all 24 declared taxon–region pairs in each of the two frozen cohort tables, and all 240 fold manifests passed checksum review. One marine animal pair was ineligible for the terrain-based environmental and dual-space comparisons, and one plant pair had only one evaluable candidate fold; the latter remained in universal-method intention-to-evaluate summaries with zero contribution from missing folds but was excluded from environmental-method paired comparisons.

For animals, mean 10-km intention-to-evaluate recall in the reconstruction was 0.0805 for frozen ACSP, 0.0776 for local-evidence-only Top-5, 0.0417 for geographic maximin, and 0.0389 for same-pool random selection. Among the 11 terrain-eligible animal pairs, recall was 0.0419 for environmental maximin and 0.0224 for dual-space maximin. ACSP exceeded random selection by 0.0416 (95% bootstrap interval 0.0098–0.0767; sign-flip p = 0.0449). The difference between local-evidence-only selection and ACSP was −0.0029 (95% interval −0.0512–0.0492; p = 0.936), providing no evidence that the 25% geographic-complementarity component added recovery beyond local ranking. Geographic maximin was lower than ACSP by 0.0388, but that paired difference was unresolved (95% interval −0.0039–0.0854 when expressed as ACSP minus geographic maximin; p = 0.164). ACSP exceeded environmental maximin by 0.0459 and dual-space maximin by 0.0655 among eligible pairs; the corresponding sign-flip p-values were 0.0149 and 0.0087.

Across the pooled 36 plant pairs in the reconstruction, ACSP and local-evidence-only ranking were identical by design, with mean recall 0.1489. Same-pool random recall was 0.1000 and geographic maximin recall was 0.1259. Among 35 terrain-eligible pairs, environmental maximin recall was 0.0957 and dual-space maximin recall was 0.1048. ACSP exceeded random selection by 0.0489 (95% interval 0.0136–0.0930; sign-flip p = 0.0112). The ACSP–geographic-maximin difference was 0.0230 but remained unresolved (95% interval −0.0042–0.0520; p = 0.116). ACSP exceeded environmental maximin by 0.0564 (95% interval 0.0150–0.1126; p = 0.0086), whereas the difference from dual-space maximin was 0.0473 with an interval extending narrowly across zero (−0.0008–0.1059; p = 0.0814).

The held-out greedy oracle remained substantially above ACSP in both taxonomic groups. Oracle recall was 0.1535 in animals and 0.2266 in plants, corresponding to mean gains over ACSP of 0.0730 and 0.0777, respectively. Thus, the regenerated candidate pools contained additional recoverable information, but the present comparisons do not identify a validated operational rule that captures all of that headroom.

### 3.5 Matched-pool fitted-SDM comparison and decision differentiation

All 24 recovered pair artifacts passed strict checksum verification and all 720 expected fold-method rows were retained. The fitted SDM produced a valid Top-5 set in 101 of 120 folds. Sixteen folds retained genuine SDM fitting or scoring failures, and three folds had no shared candidate pool for any method. Twenty-three of 24 taxon–region pairs contained at least one SDM-evaluable fold.

In the all-declared intention-to-evaluate comparison, regional recovery was essentially identical between ACSP and fitted-SDM Top-5 ranking. Across all 24 pairs, ACSP minus SDM was −0.00027 (95% bootstrap interval −0.03852–0.03675; sign-flip p = 0.989). The animal difference was −0.00204 (−0.07959–0.07152; p = 0.964) and the plant difference was 0.00149 (−0.01110–0.01381; p = 0.829). Restricting both methods to SDM-evaluable folds changed the pooled difference to 0.04083 in favor of ACSP, but uncertainty remained broad and included zero (−0.03833–0.14545; p = 0.544). These data therefore provide no evidence for a general regional-recovery advantage of ACSP over the fitted-SDM ranking rule in this cohort.

The outcome-free decision diagnostics gave a different and more informative result. Across the 101 folds in which both methods produced a valid decision, the two Top-5 sets shared a mean of 1.93 candidates and had a mean Jaccard overlap of 0.264. The complete five-candidate set was identical in only one fold. Candidate-level local evidence and fitted-SDM suitability were only moderately rank-associated (mean Spearman correlation 0.318 across 100 folds with a defined coefficient).

The score profiles showed the expected divergence in what each decision emphasized. ACSP-selected candidates had higher mean local evidence than SDM-selected candidates (0.924 versus 0.877), whereas SDM-selected candidates had higher fitted-SDM suitability than ACSP-selected candidates (0.371 versus 0.222). Descriptively, ACSP selections were also more geographically dispersed on average (mean pairwise distance 110.8 km versus 82.4 km). The animal contrast was especially clear: no evaluable animal fold produced an identical Top-5 set, mean Jaccard overlap was 0.234, and mean pairwise distance was 116.6 km for ACSP versus 66.6 km for SDM. Plant decisions were somewhat more similar but still weakly overlapping (mean Jaccard 0.292; exact agreement in one fold).

Thus, similar 10-km regional recovery did not arise because ACSP and the fitted SDM were selecting the same candidates. Under matched training information, candidate availability, and budget, they usually produced different operational field decisions.

### 3.6 Prospective global observability boundary

The one-shot global observability workflow froze 6,147 historical candidate rows and attempted 3,161 unique focal-species provider queries. Of these, 3,132 succeeded and 29 returned HTTP 429. The preregistered failure rule therefore terminated Stage 2 as `abort_not_evaluable`. No complete 96-frame artifact was created; 2021–2025 heldout facets, candidate generation, robust support, random baselines, recall and lift remained unopened or unexecuted. The terminal classification is consequently supply `protocol_abort`, hypothesis `unavailable` and promotion `not_promoted`. It does not change the validated Japanese results and is not evidence for a null or adverse observability effect.

## 4. Discussion

The original independent cross-taxon results support a bounded conclusion: the frozen ACSP policies added modest regional recovery value beyond random selection from their generated candidate pools. The effect was positive in both animal and plant confirmation samples, persisted under leave-one-pair-out analyses, and was stable across repeated pair-level resampling. Because the counterfactual used the same candidate pool, these effects cannot be attributed merely to placing candidates in a favorable geographic envelope.

The secondary comparator reconstruction sharpens the interpretation of what produced that value. The clearest repeated signal was focal-taxon local-habitat ranking. Plant ACSP is exactly the local-evidence policy, and in the secondary pooled plant reconstruction it exceeded both same-pool random selection and generic environmental maximin. The animal policy also exceeded random selection, but its 25% geographic-complementarity component was not detectably better than local-evidence-only Top-5. Consequently, the current evidence does not support complementarity itself as the demonstrated source of ACSP performance. The acronym reflects the broader design framework, but the publication claim should be centered on occurrence-conditioned local evidence and auditable finite-set selection.

The comparison with geographic maximin deserves a similarly restrained interpretation. Geographic maximin produced lower mean recall than ACSP in both animals and plants, but the pair-level differences were not resolved at conventional uncertainty thresholds. ACSP therefore cannot claim general superiority over geographically balanced same-pool selection from these data. In contrast, terrain-only environmental maximin was clearly weaker than ACSP in both taxonomic groups. This pattern is consistent with the idea that environmental coverage and focal-taxon environmental resemblance answer different survey questions: maximizing generic environmental spread is not equivalent to ranking environments that resemble observed conditions for the focal taxon.

The fitted-SDM benchmark provides the clearest test of ACSP's intended methodological positioning. It did not show that ACSP is a better distribution model; indeed, the all-declared regional-recovery difference between ACSP and fitted-SDM Top-5 ranking was essentially zero. That absence of superiority is not a problem for the framework because ACSP does not estimate the same object. A fitted SDM returns a location-indexed model score and requires a downstream rule to turn that score into a field itinerary. ACSP declares the finite candidate set as the output of the decision problem itself.

The low selection overlap makes this distinction operational rather than semantic. If ACSP were merely a renamed SDM-high rule, matching the training folds, candidate pool, and Top-5 budget should have produced highly similar decisions. Instead, the average overlap was fewer than two of five candidates, mean Jaccard similarity was 0.264, and only one of 101 evaluable folds produced the same Top-5 set. At the same time, regional recovery remained similar. This combination demonstrates that different decision policies can arrive at comparable regional recovery through different candidate sets. Similar predictive utility at one coarse endpoint therefore does not imply equivalence of estimand or decision behavior.

The relationship between the methods is better described as composition than replacement. Production ACSP can include an SDM-derived macro-model score alongside local occurrence-conditioned evidence and other declared information. Conversely, an SDM coupled to a sufficiently explicit survey-design optimizer can solve a closely related downstream decision problem. ACSP should therefore not claim exclusive ownership of survey optimization. Its contribution is the transparent occurrence-to-candidate-to-finite-decision workflow, explicit budgeted output, leakage boundary, same-pool counterfactual, and machine-readable distinction between implemented features and validated claims.

The SDM applicability results should also be interpreted cautiously. A valid fitted-SDM Top-5 set was available in 101 of 120 folds; the remainder included genuine SDM fitting/scoring failures and three folds in which the common candidate pool itself was empty. These failures are useful audit information but are not evidence that SDMs are intrinsically unsuitable for field planning. Different SDM implementations, predictors, spatial structures, background designs, or survey optimizers could behave differently. The direct benchmark establishes a comparison with one frozen production-aligned ensemble, not a theorem about the class of SDMs.

The comparator reconstruction is intentionally secondary rather than confirmatory. The taxon–region cohorts and algorithms were frozen, and comparator outcomes were not used to retune the policies. However, the standard-baseline benchmark re-fetched GBIF occurrences and regenerated spatial folds because the original candidate-level pools had not been archived. Its absolute recall values therefore differ from the original confirmation table and should not be interpreted as replication of the exact same folds. In contrast, the fitted-SDM recovery reused the original frozen artifacts from its own untouched run after rejecting invalid QA outputs; no scientific inputs were regenerated during recovery.

The oracle results also indicate that selection remains improvable. The candidate pools contained substantial headroom beyond both ACSP and generic maximin baselines. That headroom does not justify outcome-driven retuning on the present cohorts. A defensible next development cycle should instead test new decision targets on separate development data. For island surveys, especially promising extensions are environmental-support continuity, persistent survey patches, route-aware patch selection, and adaptive field feedback. These would remain downstream decision or survey-structure problems rather than attempts to create another occurrence-probability model.

The supported spatial scale is deliberately limited. Ten-kilometre recovery validates regional prioritization, not an exact search point. The 5-km plant interval crossed zero, and candidate-cell resolution places an additional technical ceiling on fine interpretation. High-resolution terrain and generic land-cover refinements tested during development did not provide transferable improvement. Finer claims will require species-relevant vegetation, substrate, moisture, host, hydrographic, or bathymetric information and new independent validation.

Several production functions remain outside the evidence base reported here. The current application can integrate macro-model outputs, access evidence, field feedback, multiple survey areas, route proxies, and alternative Discovery or Learning plans. These features may improve usability and generate testable hypotheses, but they must not be described as cross-taxon validated merely because they are implemented. The machine-readable claim matrix and frozen policy manifests are intended to keep software capabilities separate from evidential status.

The prospective global attempt adds a distinct limitation: evaluability depends on complete, reproducible provider coverage before a generalization hypothesis can be tested. Twenty-nine rate-limited historical queries were enough to invalidate the frozen 96-frame denominator under the one-shot contract. Treating that event as a protocol abort preserves the inferential boundary, but it also means the current evidence does not validate automatic geographic framing or a global name-only product. The supported software claim remains the bounded Japanese 12-region decision procedure.

Prospective validation should record every attempted site, standardized search duration and area, non-detections, access failures, weather, phenological state, observer, and survey method. Such data would permit evaluation of discoveries per search hour, detection-aware performance, access-adjusted utility, and adaptive learning. The current positive-only retrospective endpoint cannot establish occupancy, specificity, abundance, accessibility, or field efficiency.

## 5. Conclusion

ACSP converts occurrence-conditioned evidence into fixed-budget regional survey decisions and evaluates those decisions against alternatives from the same candidate pools. Independent plant and animal cohorts supported modest positive lift over random selection at 10 km, while an attempted 5-km plant claim did not replicate. A later predeclared reconstruction benchmark showed that the observed selection signal is best attributed to focal-taxon local-habitat ranking rather than to a demonstrated general benefit of geographic complementarity. A one-shot global observability attempt stopped before heldout access because historical provider coverage was incomplete; it delimits generalization but supplies no scientific effect estimate.

The direct fitted-SDM comparison clarifies the framework's position rather than establishing a winner. ACSP and a production-aligned fitted-SDM Top-5 rule achieved essentially the same all-declared 10-km regional recovery, yet their selected sets usually differed substantially. ACSP is therefore supported as an auditable occurrence-to-finite-decision layer within the validated Japanese frame, not as a replacement suitability model, a globally validated name-only product, or a universally superior alternative to SDMs. SDM output can remain one evidence source within that decision layer. The methodological boundary is the object being returned and validated: a finite field-survey decision under a declared candidate budget rather than another pointwise occurrence surface.
