Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Occurrence-conditioned robust candidate patches for biodiversity field surveys: cross-taxon confirmation and limits to geographic transfer”, for consideration as a research article in *Ecological Informatics*.

Ecologists increasingly rely on opportunistic species-occurrence data to plan field surveys, but the translation from recorded points to defensible search areas remains a distinct computational problem. We present an occurrence-conditioned method that reconstructs robust environmental support and produces bounded, non-ranked candidate patches without treating the resulting support as occupancy probability or a priority score.

The method was frozen before independent assessment across 96 taxon–region pairs and 480 spatial folds in 12 Japanese regional frames. The retained candidate patches improved mean 10-km withheld-occurrence recovery over same-size random sets by 0.08559 (95% pair-bootstrap confidence interval 0.05119–0.12165). Equally important, we report preregistered failures of country-framed generalization and a separate provider-limited confirmation that stopped before held-out evaluation, rather than presenting positive conditional estimates as universal success.

The study contributes a reproducible representation of occurrence-derived survey search units together with explicit applicability and abstention boundaries. Its emphasis on ecological data integration, spatial validation, uncertainty and reproducible computational decisions aligns with the journal's focus on ecological informatics.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
